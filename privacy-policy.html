DiagOne Privacy Policy

App: DiagOne (package com.moves5.diagonemobile) · Developer: Moves5 · Effective: October 15, 2026

DiagOne is a phone diagnostics and game-tools app. This page explains what the app does with information. In short: DiagOne has no accounts, no ads and no analytics, and it does not send your test results or personal data to the developer.

1. Information stored on your phone

Test results, your game list, settings, scores and play statistics are saved only on your device. The developer cannot see them. You can clear them by clearing the app's storage in Android Settings, or uninstalling the app.

2. Permissions and why the app asks for them

PermissionUsed for
CameraThe camera test and flashlight. Images are shown on screen only; nothing is recorded, saved or uploaded.
MicrophoneThe microphone test (live level display). Nothing is recorded, saved or uploaded.
LocationThe GPS test, to show your position on screen while the test runs. Your coordinates are not saved or uploaded by the app.
VibrationThe vibration test.
Network and Wi-Fi stateThe network check, to show your connection type and status.
Close background appsThe optional memory cleanup button in Game Zone. It asks Android to close background apps; Android may restart them.
Do Not Disturb access (optional)Only if you choose to let Game Zone switch Do Not Disturb on or off for you. You grant it in Android Settings and can withdraw it there at any time.

You can deny any permission. The related test then simply does not run; the rest of the app keeps working.

3. Installed apps

Game Zone reads the list of apps on your phone that can be opened, only so you can pick games and launch them. This list stays on your device and is never sent anywhere.

4. Internet requests made by the app

The app only contacts the internet in these two cases. In both, the server operator can see your IP address, as with any website you visit.

  • Network test. When you press a network test, the app requests a tiny page from Google's connectivity server (www.gstatic.com) to measure delay.
  • GPS map (optional, off by default). If you switch the map on, the app downloads map tiles from OpenStreetMap (tile.openstreetmap.org). That server can see which map area you are viewing, which is roughly where you are. Your exact coordinates are not sent. With the map off, nothing is requested.

These services have their own privacy policies: Google and the OpenStreetMap Foundation.

5. What we do not do

  • No user accounts or sign-in.
  • No advertising and no advertising ID use.
  • No analytics or crash-tracking libraries.
  • No selling or sharing of personal data.

6. Children

DiagOne does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children, because it does not collect personal information at all.

7. Changes to this policy

If the app's behaviour changes in a way that affects privacy, this page will be updated and the effective date above will change.
